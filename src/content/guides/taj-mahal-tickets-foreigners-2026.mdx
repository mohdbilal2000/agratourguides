Most travellers arrive at the Taj Mahal having read three different ticket prices online, none of them current, and at least one of them invented by someone selling a tour. So here is the entire cost of visiting the Taj as a foreigner in 2026, line by line, with nothing rounded off and nothing hidden. I lead this walk most mornings; these are the real numbers.

## What does the Taj Mahal ticket cost for foreigners in 2026?

The base foreigner ticket is **₹1,300** — about **$16 or €15** at current rates. That single ticket is more generous than most people realise. It already includes:

- A pair of shoe covers (mandatory on the marble platform)
- A 500ml bottle of water
- A token for the free e-rickshaw or golf cart from the ticket plaza to the gate

Petrol and diesel vehicles are banned within 500 metres of the Taj to protect the marble from acid pollution, so that last stretch is electric — and you've already paid for it. Tourists routinely pay touts ₹100–200 for a ride that their ticket already covers.

The one genuine extra is the **main mausoleum chamber** — the inner room holding the false tombs of Shah Jahan and Mumtaz Mahal. That costs **₹200** more, paid separately at a small counter inside. Everything else — the gardens, the great gate, the mosque, the jawab, the riverside platform, every photo angle — is covered by your ₹1,300.

## Who pays what? The full price tiers

| Visitor category | Base ticket (2026) | Notes |
|------------------|--------------------|-------|
| Indian national | ₹50 | Aadhaar / ID may be checked |
| SAARC & BIMSTEC national | ₹540 | Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, etc. |
| Foreign national | ₹1,300 | + ₹200 for the mausoleum |
| Child under 15 (any nationality) | Free | ID for age may be requested |

Yes, a foreigner pays 26 times what an Indian pays. This is **official, published, two-tier pricing** used at the Red Fort, Qutub Minar, Amber Fort and almost every major Indian monument. It is not a scam and it is not negotiable. Build it into your budget and move on — the monument is worth ₹1,300 several times over.

## The three gate scams that cost tourists the most

After the ticket, the real money disappears in small cons at the gate. The three most common, in order of how often I see them:

1. **The "free" guide.** A friendly man attaches himself to you, offers to "just help," then demands ₹1,000–2,000 at the end — and he never even entered the marble area, because unlicensed guides legally can't. A real guide carries a Ministry of Tourism photo licence; ask to see it before anyone walks with you.
2. **The e-rickshaw "fee."** Someone charges you ₹100–200 for the buggy to the gate. It's free. It's on your ticket.
3. **The photo "spot fee."** A man positions you at the classic bench, takes your phone, shoots, then asks for ₹500. Your guide takes those same photos for free — it's part of the service, and it's why our reviews are full of "amazing photos."

## What does a complete Taj visit actually cost?

For one foreign traveller who wants to do it properly — base ticket, mausoleum, a shared licensed guide, and nothing wasted on touts:

| Item | Cost (₹) | Cost ($) |
|------|----------|----------|
| Base foreigner ticket | 1,300 | ~16 |
| Mausoleum chamber | 200 | ~2.5 |
| Licensed guide (₹1,800 ÷ 5 people) | 360 | ~4.5 |
| E-rickshaw, water, shoe covers | 0 (included) | 0 |
| **Total per person** | **~1,860** | **~$23** |

Solo travellers pay the full ₹1,800 guide fee, so budget closer to ₹3,300 ($40). A couple splitting a guide pays about ₹2,400 ($29) each. None of this includes getting to Agra — see our guide on [reaching Agra from Delhi](/guides/delhi-to-agra-how-to-reach/) for that.
